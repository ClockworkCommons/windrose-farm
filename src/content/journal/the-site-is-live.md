Windrose.farm is live, which feels slightly premature given that we have not
yet grown anything.

That is rather the point. If we waited until there was a harvest to
photograph, the most interesting part would already be over — the part where
the ground is hard, the plans are untested, and nobody involved knows yet
which ideas are going to work.

## What's here

- **[Our Story](/story/)** — where this came from, including Aunt Rosie,
  whose beekeeping and gardening started all of this long before there was
  any land to put it on.
- **[The Farm](/farm/)** — the four things we are slowly building: an
  apiary, gardens, an orchard, and a small vineyard. Mostly intentions at
  this stage, honestly described as such.
- **[The Land](/land/)** — the technical reality. Soil survey data, water
  systems, infrastructure, and the five-phase plan. This is the page with
  the uncomfortable numbers on it.
- **[The Seasons](/seasons/)** — what the land does on its own, through the
  year. Wildflowers nobody planted, monsoon skies, and a night sky worth the
  drive by itself.
- **The journal** — this. Notes as we go.

## What to expect

Not very much, very slowly, for a long time. That is the honest forecast.

We will write when something actually happens: a mound built, a swale cut,
a tree in the ground, a hive installed. Some of those entries will describe
things that worked. Others will describe things that did not, and those are
the ones worth reading — a farm blog where everything succeeds is a farm
blog that is leaving something out.

There is nothing for sale here, and there may never be. If honey or fruit
ever comes of this in quantities beyond what the family can eat, we will
mention it. That is not why the site exists.

## Why bother

Partly to keep an honest record for ourselves — in ten years it will be
useful to know exactly how little we understood at the start.

Partly for anyone else staring at a hard piece of ground and wondering
whether it is worth the trouble. We do not know yet either. But we are
going to find out in public, and share the working.

Thanks for reading. Come back in a season or two — there should be
something new by then.
